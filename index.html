Nothing is scheduled to happen on 1 January 2028. No AGI launch, no UBI bill, no economic event. The mechanism

Six steps, none of which require a robot uprising

01
Capability compounds

Each model generation ships faster than the last, and increasingly ships the next one. The curve does not need to be exponential to outrun retraining.

02
Knowledge work automates

Not all of it, and not at once. Enough of the entry rungs go first that the ladder loses its bottom half.

03
Labour decouples from value

Output rises while the wage share falls. The experience premium — the thing you spent a decade buying — compresses toward zero.

04
Owners capture the upside

Whoever holds equity, compute, land or tokens collects the productivity gain. Whoever holds a job collects a severance.

05
Transfers replace wages

Some form of support arrives, because the alternative is unrest. Support is a floor. It has never been a ladder.

06
The door closes

Assets are now priced off automated cash flows. You can no longer buy in with labour, because labour is what stopped being worth anything.

The only escape route anyone across the whole argument agrees on: own a piece before step six.

D4Diligence

Methodology

How 31 December 2027 was set

The original site states the date came from a Grok 4.1 forecast built on 237 datapoints, published 12 February 2026, and describes it as the estimated deadline to build scalable, independent generational wealth — through businesses, equity or assets — before widespread knowledge-work automation and the UBI/policy response to it lock in low economic mobility.

The year is not arbitrary. 2027 is the load-bearing date in the broader Silicon Valley argument about advanced AI, popularised by Leopold Aschenbrenner's Situational Awareness and echoed across the forecasting community. The countdown borrows that inflection and gives it a wall clock.

What a forecast is not: a schedule. There is no announced AGI launch, no legislated UBI programme and no economic event on 1 January 2028. Treat the number the way you would treat a hurricane cone — the direction is the information, the exact landfall is not.

Reasonable people disagree, and their disagreement is in the receipts: Jevons' paradox, Baumol's cost disease, comparative advantage, and the plain historical record that every previous automation wave raised wages in the sectors it did not touch. The honest version of this thesis is a probability, not a prophecy — which is also why the clock on this page is rendered in ink rather than red.
